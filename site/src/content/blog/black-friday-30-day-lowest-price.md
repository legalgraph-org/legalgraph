Black Friday is a day of price reductions, so it is worth knowing how German law wants them shown. The
rule is short. Anyone in Germany who announces a price
reduction to consumers must also state the **lowest price they charged in the 30 days before the
reduction**. Since the EU Court of Justice's *Aldi Süd* judgment of
26 September 2024, the advertised reduction itself, such as "−30 %", must also be **calculated from that
price**.

This article goes through the rule, its exceptions and four worked examples. Every statement links to
the provision or the judgment in legalgraph, where you can read the official text. Where we interpret,
we say so. It covers German law only.

## The rule

[§ 11(1) of the Price Indication Ordinance (PAngV)](/id/DE-PAngV-s11-1):

> Anyone obliged to state a total price shall, in every announcement of a price reduction for goods,
> state to consumers the lowest total price it applied to consumers within the last 30 days before
> applying the price reduction.

German original: "Wer zur Angabe eines Gesamtpreises verpflichtet ist, hat gegenüber Verbrauchern bei
jeder Bekanntgabe einer Preisermäßigung für eine Ware den niedrigsten Gesamtpreis anzugeben, den er
innerhalb der letzten 30 Tage vor der Anwendung der Preisermäßigung gegenüber Verbrauchern angewendet
hat."

Four things in that sentence decide whether it applies to you:

- **"Anyone obliged to state a total price"**: a trader who offers goods or services to consumers, or
  advertises to them with prices ([§ 3(1) PAngV](/id/DE-PAngV-s3-1)).
- **"every announcement of a price reduction"**: a crossed-out price, "−30 %", "now only", a red
  sale badge. Whatever tells the consumer that the price has gone down.
- **"for goods"**: the paragraph speaks of goods. It says nothing about services, and we don't
  either.
- **"to consumers"**: business-to-business offers are not covered.

The rule comes from EU law. [Article 6a(1)](/id/EU-1998-6-art6a-1) and
[(2)](/id/EU-1998-6-art6a-2) of the Price Indication Directive (98/6/EC), added by the Omnibus Directive
in 2019, require every announcement of a price reduction to indicate the "prior price", defined as "the
lowest price applied by the trader during a period of time not shorter than 30 days prior to the
application of the price reduction". Germany chose exactly 30 days.

In legalgraph this is the rule [Show the lowest price of the last 30 days with every price
reduction](/id/DE-R-prior-price). It applies to traders who compete for consumers on the German market,
which in our reading includes shops established abroad that sell to people in Germany: unfair-competition
law follows the market where competitors meet ([Article 6(1) Rome II](/id/EU-2007-864-art6-1)).

## What Aldi Süd added: the percentage counts from the 30-day price

The German rule only says that the 30-day lowest price must be *shown*. The case shows why that wasn't
enough. In October 2022, an Aldi Süd brochure advertised bananas at €1.29 and pineapples at €1.49, each
next to a struck-through €1.69, the bananas with a percentage reduction and the pineapples with a "price
highlight" label. Underneath stood "Last selling price. Lowest price in the last 30 days": €1.29 for the
bananas and €1.39 for the pineapples (judgment, para. 11). The bananas were "reduced" to exactly their
30-day lowest price; the pineapples cost more than theirs. A consumer association (Verbraucherzentrale
Baden-Württemberg) sued, and the Regional Court of Düsseldorf asked the EU Court of Justice what the
Directive requires.

The Court answered in [C-330/23, *Aldi Süd*](/id/CJEU-C-330-23-ruling-1) that Article 6a(1) and (2):

> must be interpreted as meaning that it requires that a price reduction of a product announced by a
> trader in the form of a percentage, or in the form of a promotional statement intended to highlight
> the advantageous nature of the announced price, must be determined on the basis of the 'prior price',
> within the meaning of paragraph 2 of that article.

So it is not enough to print the 30-day lowest price somewhere next to an offer. **The reduction you
advertise, as a percentage or as a claim like "best price", has to be measured against it.** A shop
cannot show the lowest price in a footnote and calculate its "−50 %" from a higher "regular" price.

## Four examples

The prices are invented. Black Friday is 27 November 2026, so the 30 days before it run from
28 October.

**1. Correct.** A jacket sold at €80 since October is reduced to €60 on Black Friday.

> ~~€80~~ **€60** −25 %
> Lowest price in the last 30 days: €80

The 30-day lowest price is €80, and −25 % is calculated from €80.

**2. Wrong: price raised before the sale.** The jacket cost €80 until 9 November, was raised to €120 on
10 November, and is advertised on Black Friday as "~~€120~~ **€60** −50 %".

The lowest price in the 30 days before the reduction is €80, not €120. Under § 11(1) the shop must show
€80, and under *Aldi Süd* the percentage must be calculated from €80, which makes it −25 %. "−50 %"
is measured from the wrong price.

**3. Wrong: an earlier one-day deal.** The jacket normally costs €80 but was sold for €55 on a one-day
promotion on 11 November. On Black Friday it is advertised as "**€60** −25 %".

The lowest price in the last 30 days is €55. Measured from €55, €60 is not a reduction at all, so in
our reading it cannot be announced as one. The shop can sell the jacket for €60, but not as a Black
Friday price cut.

**4. A reduction that grows during Black Week.** The jacket is €80. From Monday it is −10 %, from
Wednesday −20 %, from Friday −30 %, without a break.

[§ 11(2) PAngV](/id/DE-PAngV-s11-2) allows this case: during a progressive price reduction that
increases without interruption, the shop may keep showing the lowest price from **before the first
step**, here €80. It doesn't have to recalculate from Monday's or Wednesday's price. If the reduction
stops in between and starts again, the exception no longer fits the wording ("ohne Unterbrechung",
without interruption).

## Who else is covered, and what is exempt

- **Unit-price-only traders.** [§ 11(3)](/id/DE-PAngV-s11-3) applies the rule to those who, under § 4(2)
  PAngV, only have to state the unit price.
- **Individual price reductions** are exempt ([§ 11(4) No. 1](/id/DE-PAngV-s11-4)). The ordinance
  doesn't define "individual"; in our reading it means a reduction for a particular customer, not a
  discount announced to everyone.
- **Perishable goods** are exempt when the price is cut because they are about to spoil or reach their
  best-before date, and the shop makes that clear to consumers in a suitable way
  ([§ 11(4) No. 2](/id/DE-PAngV-s11-4)).

## The other price rules still apply

A Black Friday ad is still a price ad:

- the **total price** must be stated
  ([§ 3(1) PAngV](/id/DE-PAngV-s3-1), rule [State total prices](/id/DE-R-total-price));
- goods sold by weight, volume, length or area also need the **unit price**
  ([§ 4(1) PAngV](/id/DE-PAngV-s4-1), rule [State unit prices](/id/DE-R-unit-price)).

## What happens if a shop gets it wrong

German unfair-competition law treats a breach of a statutory rule that regulates market conduct as
unfair when it is liable to appreciably harm consumers or competitors
([§ 3a UWG](/id/DE-UWG-s3a)). In our reading the price rules are such rules, so a wrong reduction can
be pursued as unfair competition. legalgraph doesn't yet hold the fines the
ordinance provides for, so this article names no amounts.

## Checklist before Black Friday

- [ ] For every reduced product, look up the lowest price you charged consumers in the 30 days before
      the reduction starts
- [ ] Show that price with every announcement of the reduction: product page, category tile, banner,
      newsletter
- [ ] Calculate every percentage and every "best price" claim from that price
- [ ] Don't announce a reduction where the new price isn't below that price
- [ ] For a reduction that grows during the week: no gaps between the steps
- [ ] Total price and, where needed, unit price are shown as usual

## What this article does not cover

- **Austria and Switzerland**, whose price rules are not in legalgraph yet.
- **Fines** under the ordinance.
- **Services**: § 11 speaks of goods.
- **Comparisons with a manufacturer's recommended retail price** in general. *Aldi Süd* settles only
  that an advertised reduction must be measured from the 30-day lowest price.
